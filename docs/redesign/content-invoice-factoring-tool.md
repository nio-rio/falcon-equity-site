## What it helps
- Label: What it helps
- Heading: When factoring is the right fit.
- Making payroll while invoices sit unpaid
	- Sell those receivables and the cash is here now, instead of arriving after payday has already gone out.
- Taking on a contract bigger than your cash on hand
	- Factoring can fund materials and labour against invoices from customers who are good for the money.
- Smoothing clients who pay slowly
	- When customers take their time and suppliers do not, an ongoing facility keeps the gap from turning into a crisis.
- Growing without taking on more debt
	- Factoring is a sale of invoices, not a loan. It brings cash in without adding a repayment to the balance sheet.

## When factoring isn't the right fit.
- Label: When factoring isn't the right fit.
- Heading: Factoring is priced against your invoices and your customers, so it only works when those two things are strong. Here are three situations where another Falcon product fits better.
- If you sell to consumers, not businesses
	- Factoring needs outstanding B2B invoices from creditworthy commercial or government customers. Consumer sales do not qualify.
	- Link: Merchant Cash Advance → /services/merchant-cash-advance
- If your customers already pay you quickly
	- There is little point selling an invoice that was about to be paid anyway. A line of credit covers a short gap without discounting the money you are owed.
	- Link: Business Line of Credit → /services/business-line-of-credit
- If you need money for something other than waiting on receivables
	- When the cost is a purchase or a project rather than a timing gap, a term loan spreads it over a schedule you choose.
	- Link: Business Term Loan → /services/business-term-loan

## FAQ (expandable)
- Label: FAQ · Heading: Invoice factoring questions
- Is factoring a loan? No. Factoring is the sale of your receivables at a discount. Approval leans heavily on the creditworthiness of your customers.
- Which invoices qualify? Outstanding B2B invoices from creditworthy commercial or government customers are the strongest fit.
- Is it a one-time deal? It can be ongoing. Many businesses use factoring as a continuing facility that grows with their sales.
- What does it cost? Falcon charges a 0% agreement fee. Factoring is priced as a discount on the invoices you sell, which your specialist discloses before you sign. No specific rate is published on this page.
- How fast can I get funded? Timing depends on the invoices and how quickly you send your documents. Your specialist will tell you what to expect for your file.
- What do I need to apply? Six months of business bank statements, a government-issued photo ID, a voided check, a list of any open advances or loans, and the outstanding invoices you want to factor.
