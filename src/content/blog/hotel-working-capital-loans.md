Hotels, inns and other hospitality businesses earn much of their year in a few busy months. The rest of the year, rooms sit emptier, but payroll, utilities, insurance and loan payments don't take a break.

**Hotel working capital loans** help bridge those months, fund refreshes before peak and cover repairs that can't wait. This guide covers how lenders look at seasonal properties and how to choose the right funding.

## How lenders read a seasonal property

Underwriters mostly read your business bank statements. For a seasonal hotel, those statements show big months and small months. That's not a problem in itself, but it needs context.

What helps:

- **Twelve months of history**, so the full season is visible.
- **A simple monthly summary** of occupancy and revenue, showing that the pattern repeats.
- **Forward bookings**, if you have them, for the next peak.
- **An explanation of one-offs**, like a month closed for renovation.

Without context, a slow month can look like a business in trouble. With context, it looks like a business that knows itself. Read [what your bank statements say](/blog/what-your-bank-statements-say) for what underwriters notice.

## Timing your application

The best time to apply is often during or just after peak season, when recent deposits are strongest. Funding arranged then is ready for the slow months. Waiting until cash is already tight usually means fewer options and worse terms.

## The right tool for each need

### Off-season overhead and deposits: line of credit

A [Business Line of Credit](/services/business-line-of-credit) is usually the best fit for repeating seasonal gaps:

- **Amounts:** $10,000–$500,000
- **Terms:** Revolving
- **Requirements:** 6+ months in business, $10K+ monthly revenue, an active bank account

Draw in the slow months, repay in peak and draw again next year. It also works for deposits a tour operator or event planner pays to secure blocks before travelers pay in full.

### Room refreshes and repairs: equipment financing

Beds, furniture, linens, in-room technology, kitchen equipment, HVAC units and shuttle vans last for years. [Collateral & Asset Loans](/services/collateral-asset-loans) spread their cost over that life:

- **Amounts:** $10,000–$1,000,000
- **Terms:** 12–60 months
- **Requirements:** an equipment quote or invoice, 6+ months in business, an active business

Timing matters: arrange financing in the off-season so rooms are refreshed before peak, when they'll earn the most. Ask suppliers to itemize delivery and installation. Our [equipment break-even guide](/blog/equipment-financing-break-even) helps test whether a refresh pays for itself.

### Larger, defined projects: term loan

For a planned renovation, adding a restaurant or bar, or a new property's opening costs, a [Business Term Loan](/services/business-term-loan) gives a lump sum and a fixed payment:

- **Amounts:** $10,000–$2,000,000
- **Terms:** 6–36 months
- **Requirements:** 6+ months in business, $15K+ monthly revenue, 550+ credit score

Before committing, check that the fixed payment fits your slowest month, not your average one.

### Fast but expensive: merchant cash advance

A [Merchant Cash Advance](/services/merchant-cash-advance) ($5,000–$5,000,000, 3–18 months, 3+ months in business) can fund quickly, and repayment as a share of sales means smaller payments in slow weeks. That sounds like a good fit for seasonality, but be careful: **it is expensive**, and the off-season is when margins are thinnest. Taking one in the slow months to cover overhead can leave you paying it back through your next peak instead of building reserves.

If you consider one, read the estimated APR disclosure New York requires on sales-based financing ([23 NYCRR 600.7](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/23-NYCRR-600.7)) and our [factor rate guide](/blog/how-to-read-a-factor-rate). If you already carry several, [Consolidation of Positions](/services/consolidation-of-positions) can combine them and aims to cut current daily or weekly payments, in some cases by 30%–50%.

## Travel agencies and tour operators

Agencies and tour operators have a different version of the same problem. They often pay suppliers, like hotels, transport and guides, before or around the time travelers pay. A line of credit can cover those deposits.

If you bill corporate clients on invoice terms, for example for corporate travel or events, the [Invoice Factoring Tool](/services/invoice-factoring-tool) may also fit ($10,000–$3,000,000, ongoing facility, B2B invoices from creditworthy customers, 3+ months in business). Consumer bookings paid by card aren't factorable invoices.

## Repairs that can't wait

Some costs don't respect the calendar. A failed boiler in winter, a roof leak before a sold-out weekend or a broken walk-in in a property restaurant can take rooms or revenue offline at once. For these, speed matters, but so does structure. If the fix is a piece of equipment with a long life, like an HVAC unit or a water heater, equipment financing spreads the cost appropriately, and an undrawn line of credit can cover the deposit or labor while the financing is arranged. Keeping a line open, even if you rarely draw it, is one of the cheapest forms of insurance against this kind of emergency. Make a short list of your property's most failure-prone systems and get rough replacement quotes now, so you're not starting from zero when something breaks.

## Build a seasonal cash plan

Financing works best alongside a plan. A simple version:

1. **List fixed monthly costs** that continue all year.
2. **Estimate revenue by month** from last year and current bookings.
3. **Find the gap** in the slow months. That's your target line size, plus a cushion.
4. **Set aside reserves in peak** so you rely less on credit each year.
5. **Schedule refreshes and repairs** for the off-season, financed so they don't drain reserves.

Run payment scenarios in the [loan calculator](/resources/loan-calculator).

## Preparing your file

- Twelve months of business bank statements, complete PDFs
- A monthly occupancy and revenue summary
- Forward bookings or group contracts, if any
- Owner ID and business details
- For equipment, itemized vendor quotes
- A list of existing loans, leases and advances

See the [funding checklist](/blog/24-48-hour-funding-checklist) for the full list.

## The short version

Explain your seasonality, apply when deposits are strongest and match each need to its tool: a line for off-season gaps, equipment financing for refreshes and a term loan for larger projects.
