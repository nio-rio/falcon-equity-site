When you need capital, two of the most common options are a **[merchant cash advance (MCA)](/services/merchant-cash-advance)** and a **[business term loan](/services/business-term-loan)**. Both can fund quickly. Both can help you grow. But they are built for different situations, and choosing the wrong one can cost you.

Here is a side-by-side look.

## The quick comparison

| | Merchant Cash Advance | Business Term Loan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **What it is** | A purchase of your future receivables | A loan with a set repayment schedule |
| **Typical amounts** | $5,000 – $5,000,000 | $10,000 – $2,000,000 |
| **Typical terms** | 3 – 18 months | 6 – 36 months |
| **Typical speed** | Same day | About 2 days (term) · same day (short-term) |
| **Time in business** | 3+ months | 6+ months |
| **Monthly revenue** | $10K+ | $15K+ |
| **Credit** | Weighed less heavily | 550+ typically |
| **Best for** | Speed and short-term needs | Planned, longer-term investments |

*Ranges reflect Falcon Equity Group's current products. Final amounts and terms depend on underwriting.*

## How a merchant cash advance works

An MCA is **not a loan**. A funder purchases a portion of your future sales at a discount and gives you a lump sum today. You then remit an agreed share of your receivables, often daily or weekly, until the purchased amount is delivered.

**Where an MCA wins:**

- **Speed.** Approval leans on your revenue and bank activity, so decisions can come quickly.
- **Accessibility.** Younger businesses (3+ months) and owners with less-than-perfect credit may qualify.
- **Short-term needs.** Great for a time-sensitive inventory deal, an urgent repair or bridging a slow season.

**What to watch:**

- The **total cost** of an MCA is often higher than a traditional loan, so it is best for short-term uses that generate a return.
- Frequent payments affect daily cash flow. Avoid stacking multiple advances. If you already have, see our guide on [Consolidation of Positions](/services/consolidation-of-positions).

## How a business term loan works

A term loan gives you a **lump sum** that you repay over a set period. Payments are predictable, which makes planning easier.

**Where a term loan wins:**

- **Longer runway.** Terms up to 36 months spread out the cost of bigger investments.
- **Predictability.** A set schedule makes budgeting simpler than a daily remittance.
- **Planned growth.** Ideal for expansion, a new location or a facility remodel.

**What to watch:**

- Requirements are **stricter**: typically 6+ months in business, $15K+ monthly revenue and a 550+ credit score.
- The process can ask for a bit more documentation than an MCA.

## So which one wins?

It depends on the move you are making.

**Choose a merchant cash advance if:**

- You need funding **fast** for a specific, short-term opportunity.
- Your business is **newer** or your credit is still being built.
- The use of funds will **pay for itself quickly**, such as inventory that sells in weeks.

**Choose a business term loan if:**

- You are making a **planned investment** that pays off over months or years.
- You want **predictable, fixed payments**.
- Your business meets the **time-in-business, revenue and credit** requirements.

Still not sure? A [Business Line of Credit](/services/business-line-of-credit) can be a smart middle ground if your needs come and go.
